About the Program

Where Students Experience Real Parliamentary Democracy

The Students Parliament on TV offers students valuable hands-on experience with the workings and processes of the Senate, Chamber, House of Representatives, and State Houses of Assembly. This is not a classroom exercise — it is an authentic encounter with Nigeria's legislative heritage.

Through this program, students are inspired to cultivate confidence, discipline, patriotism, and leadership skills, while gaining a deeper understanding of their rights and responsibilities as citizens within their Students Parliament Club in their respective schools.

Additionally, outstanding students earn prizes for exceptional performance during interschool Parliamentarians contests and earn the prestigious opportunity to serve as a Day Parliamentarian in any Chamber.

  • Interschool Parliamentarians Contest
  • Day Parliamentarian Experience in Real Chambers
  • Medals and Prizes for Outstanding Performance
  • Student Parliament Club in Every School

What Students Gain

Four Pillars of the Program

Confidence

Students stand before cameras, address chambers formally, and project their voice with conviction — building unshakeable confidence that lasts beyond school.

Discipline

Parliamentary procedure demands order, timing, and respect. Students learn to follow rules, listen attentively, and conduct themselves with exceptional maturity.

Patriotism

By participating in Nigeria's legislative processes, students develop a deep sense of pride in their nation and a commitment to contributing positively to society.

Leadership

From moving motions to representing peers on issues of education, child welfare, and youth development — students emerge as genuine community leaders.

Day Speaker Programme

How to Win a Day Speaker Spot

Earning a "Day Speaker" position in the Students Parliament TV program is an honour that demands preparation, presence, and purpose. Master these principles and you stand apart from the floor.

01
Master Parliamentary Procedures
Speak with bold confidence, present clear solutions to student issues, and maintain exceptional composure under studio lights or live broadcast pressure.
02
Respect the Hierarchy of the Floor
Address the house formally, acknowledge the Speaker correctly, and always respect the order of proceedings and the seniority of those on the floor.
03
Control Your Timing
Keep your contributions precise and purposeful. Avoid rambling during proceedings — every word you deliver on camera must count.
04
Deliver a Powerful Presentation
Stand straight, make direct eye contact with the camera, and project your voice with authority. Your posture and presence speak before your words do.
05
Use Persuasive, Simple Language
Use words that are clear, compelling, and accessible. The most powerful arguments are those any student in any school can understand and believe in.
06
Back Arguments with Facts
Ground your contributions in logical facts about education, youth development, child rights, or student welfare. Evidence elevates every debate.
07
Speak on Real Student Issues
Focus on matters that affect Nigerian students — school infrastructure, learning tools, student welfare, child protection, child trafficking, and out-of-school children.
08
Offer Actionable Solutions
Do not merely identify problems — propose realistic, implementable solutions. A speaker who solves wins the house.
09
Display Leadership Qualities
Show maturity, composure, and the ability to represent your peers with dignity. Respect opposing views during debates — it demonstrates true character.
10
Dress with Purpose
Appear in a neat, pressed school uniform or official parliamentary attire. Your presentation begins the moment you are seen — long before you speak.
11
Stay Calm Under Pressure
Studio lights, cameras, and a live audience test your composure. The students who remain calm and smile through pressure are the ones who win trust and votes.
12
Win the Audience and Judges
Your credibility, consistency, and command of the floor should make both the judges and your fellow students confident that you truly represent the voice of the next generation.
Essential Parliamentary Terms to Master
"Mr. Speaker" "Point of Order" "Motion to Move" "I So Move" "I So Submit" "I Rest My Case" "I So Support" "I Second the Motion" "Order, Order" "The Motion is Carried" "The Ayes Have It" "I Yield the Floor"
